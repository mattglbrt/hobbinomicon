I've said versions of this before and then quietly let it slide. This time I've got dates, so it's going in writing. 2027 is my Oldhammer year.

## Why Now

The honest answer is accountability. I know what works on me by now. Leave me alone with a project and no deadline and I will happily rearrange boxes for eight months and call it hobby. Put me on a list with seventy other people and a due date at the end of every month and I actually finish things. It's the same reason the daily vlog worked. Showing up is easier when somebody notices you didn't.

There are two Oldhammer challenges that do exactly that. They both run roughly January into summer, they both come back every year, and between them they cover the two things I've been meaning to paint since I was a kid. The calendar is already built. I just have to get on it.

## The Old World Army Challenge

[OWAC](https://oldworldarmychallenge.blogspot.com/) is the big one. Iannick Martin set it up to push Oldhammer painters into actually finishing an army, and it's grown to something like seventy challengers from Japan to Canada. Six months, an army of around 1,000 points, Warhammer Fantasy or Rogue Trader, your choice.

The structure is the part I like. The six months break into four Rank and File months, where you owe ten foot models or five cavalry or a vehicle, plus one Leader month for your general or a suitably nasty monster, plus one Wild Card month you can spend however you want or skip entirely. Points come off 3rd or 4th edition lists. You can prime before the start date but that's it, no paint on the model before January.

There's an entry fee and it isn't money. You paint one Oldhammer figure for one of the Overlords. That's the toll.

It's also explicitly not a competition. Nobody wins. Everyone is just trying to get across the line, which is the only kind of painting event I have ever done well at.

## The 40k 2nd Edition Army Challenge

[40k2ndAC](https://cowabungajournal.com/40k2ndac-vii/) is the same idea pointed straight at 1990s 40k. A thousand points of 2nd edition over six months, about 200 points a month, with one mulligan month built in for when real life turns up. Submit five updates out of six and you've finished. It's run by Dr. The Viking, Wachtmeister and Pygmy Hippo, which is the most Oldhammer sentence I have typed all year.

This year's run had themed months, including one where you paint red and leopard print because that is simply what the 90s were, and one for sticking a pop culture reference on a banner. There's also a shared Hobby Bingo board with objectives like "paint models owned for 10+ years" and "restore or repaint an old, badly painted model." Those two are aimed directly at me and I don't appreciate it.

## The Obvious Problem

They overlap. This year OWAC ran January to June and the 40k challenge ran February to July. So I'm looking at two 1,000-point armies more or less in parallel, on top of the [Tomb Kings army I already said I'm painting in 2027](/news/bonezone-2026-open/).

Some math is required. Either the Tomb Kings force doubles as one of my challenge armies or 2027 is a much longer year than I remember. Worth flagging, because I got this wrong myself at first: the Old World Army Challenge is an *Oldhammer* thing, 3rd and 4th edition, which is not the same as the current Warhammer: The Old World game the Tomb Kings kits come from. Two very similar names, two different piles of lead.

## When To Watch

Neither 2027 edition is announced yet, and both of the pages above will update when they are.

Going by this year, the shape is: OWAC wants an introduction post with your army list and photos of the bare, unpainted models in by the end of December, and the 40k challenge wants a 1,000-point list plus a starting photo emailed in during the first week of January. Seats on the 40k one are limited and the hosts spend January sorting through pledges, so late is the same as no.

Which gives me until roughly Christmas to decide what I'm painting. In practice that means I'll decide on December 30th.

I'll post the lists here once I've committed to them. If you've run either of these before, tell me what you'd do differently, because I'd rather hear it from you now than work it out the hard way in March.

And keep painting.
