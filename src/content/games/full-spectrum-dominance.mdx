## What is Full Spectrum Dominance

*Full Spectrum Dominance*, or FSD, is "a miniature wargame for epic small-scale sci-fi miniatures." It was created by [Giacomo "Jack" Pantalone](/people/giacomo-pantalone/), known online as The Lazy Forger, and [Federico "Feo" Valsecchi](/people/federico-valsecchi/). Soon after its first release it won the OnTableTop Choice Award 2022, and it was crowdfunded in February 2023.

It's a combined-arms game. Vehicles and mechs move one at a time, and infantry move in small teams, either as grouped figures or multi-based stands. The pitch is that you fit two armies and a whole battlefield on a coffee table, as small as 2 × 3 feet.

The setting is a not-too-distant future with no faster-than-light travel. There are six playable factions: the Tech, the Enlisted, the Union, the Conglomerate, the Reclaimed and the Corsairs.

## How it plays

- **Unit cards.** Every unit is a card with four stats: Command, Defense, Save and Move. Damage is tracked on the card too.
- **Activation dice.** Each player has a pool of 12 six-sided Activation Dice. You roll them at the start of a round, then take turns spending them to activate units, fire special actions and play Support cards. The number rolled matters, since some card slots only take certain results.
- **Command.** A unit with a Command value can activate others with it as a coordinated group.
- **Reserves.** Part of your force starts off the table, and bringing in reinforcements is part of the game.
- **Mixed combat dice.** Weapons roll different types and numbers of dice (d6 up to d12) to cover a wide range of weapons and targets.
- **Distance Units.** Everything is measured in DUs, which you set to suit your scale. For 6–8mm figures 1 DU is 3 inches.

A first game is a 40-point list. Normal missions are 60 points, and the Blitz format is a quicker game without the reinforcement rules.

## How to start

1. **Get the rulebook.** It's a $10 PDF on [MyMiniFactory](https://www.myminifactory.com/object/3d-print-full-spectrum-dominance-rulebook-262512). It's a living rulebook, and buyers download each new version for free. Version 1.7 came out on 5 May 2026. The designers say a physical release is in the works for later in 2026.
2. **Use the minis you have.** FSD works at any scale from 3mm to 15mm and over, and the site says you can play with any models or even cardboard tokens.
3. **Or print the official range.** The Lazy Forger sells the models and a lot of terrain as STL files on [MyMiniFactory](https://www.myminifactory.com/users/TheLazyForger). They're about 1:300 to 1:250, with humans around 8mm tall. There's a free pre-supported 6mm sample, and the site gives 140% for 10mm and 240% for 15mm. No printer? The site keeps a list of [authorized printers](https://fsd-wargame.com/authorized-3d-printers/).
4. **Gather dice and tokens.** Each player wants 12 Activation Dice in their own colour, plus at least six d6, three d8, three d10 and two d12 for combat. Any tokens will do.
5. **Clear a small table.** 2' × 3' for a standard game with 6–8mm minis, or 2' × 2' while you learn.

The [Getting Started page](https://fsd-wargame.com/getting-started/) has recommended Blitz lists, and the [resources page](https://fsd-wargame.com/resources/) links the official army builder and a quick reference sheet.
