## What is Cosmic Horror

*Cosmic Horror* is a narrative skirmish game from [Spectre Miniatures](/studios/spectre-miniatures/), the Nottingham studio behind [Spectre Operations](/games/spectre-operations/). It's set in "the weird world of Lovecraft's 1930s America," specifically a weird 1930s New England, where ancient beings are pushing through the thin veil of reality and cults, fanatics and scholars are helping them do it.

You don't field an army. You lead an Assembly, Spectre's word for a warband, of 4 to 12 models. Preview factions include the F.B.S.T., the Order of the Salted Tongue, the Bureau of Esoteric Research, the Dreamtime of Cthulhu and the Armitage Society. Guns exist, but they don't do much against the old things. Spectre's own summary: "Cosmic Horror is not a game about winning a firefight. It is a game about what your Subjects are willing to do, and what it costs them to do it."

The game ran on Kickstarter from March 17 to April 16, 2026, and 388 backers funded it well past its goal. Stretch goals pushed the rulebook past 250 pages and added extra scenarios and Solo Play rules. Spectre lists it as coming late 2026.

This is the second go at the name. Spectre's first Cosmic Horror release was a core box with an A5 rules pamphlet that needed the Spectre Operations rulebook to play. That box is sold out. The Kickstarter game is built around a system Spectre calls Resolve.

## How it plays

- **Resolve pays for everything.** Each model spends Resolve to take Actions: move, run, fight, shoot, investigate. Subjects have 2 Resolve, Protagonists have 3. Most Actions cost 1, specialist ones cost 2 or more.
- **Protagonists and Subjects.** Protagonists lead the Assembly and pick situational Strengths before the game. Subjects are specialists. The F.B.S.T. Field Agent is built for violence and countering the Mythos, while the Order of the Salted Tongue Museum Curator can manipulate the Mythos Deck and create temporary Mythos Locations.
- **No points.** Balance comes from Assembly Capacity, where each Subject takes up slots in your roster, and a Carry Capacity for how much a model can haul.
- **Investigate to win.** Mythos Locations come in three types: Major, Minor and Living. Investigating them earns Fragments, scraps of knowledge you spend on Understanding.
- **Horror and Corruption.** In the Horror Phase fear builds up on individual models, and the Cosmic Horror Track, running 0 to 10, climbs for the whole table. As it rises, reality decays, strange effects kick in and Entities manifest. You can push the track higher on purpose to get an edge.
- **Mythos Deck and Entities.** A card deck brings the Mythos into play, and Entities and Avatars of the Old Ones can turn up.
- **One-offs or a campaign.** In a campaign, models that get wounded or taken out can pick up permanent Weaknesses.

## How to start

1. **Read the free Quick Play Rules.** The [Quick Play PDF](https://www.spectreminiatures.com/products/cosmic-horror-quick-play-rules) is free. It's two or three models a side, the Move, Run, Engagement, Melee and Investigation actions, one Minor Mythos Location and one Mythos Subject. Spectre says the mechanics are unchanged from the full game, just with the advanced systems stripped out.
2. **Wait for the rulebook.** The full rules aren't on sale outside the Kickstarter yet. The campaign offered the book on its own, digital sets with STL files, and physical sets with miniatures, all priced in pounds.
3. **Look at the existing range.** Spectre's [Cosmic Horror collection](https://www.spectreminiatures.com/collections/cosmichorror) already sells figures from the first release, such as Cosmic Horror Agents ($30), New England Police ($30), a Shoggoth ($18), the Major Horror ($38) and Standing Stones ($15).
