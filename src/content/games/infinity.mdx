## What is Infinity

Infinity is a 28mm sci-fi skirmish game by [Corvus Belli](/studios/corvus-belli/), set in the Human Sphere. That's a near-future where humanity has spread across the stars, split into rival superpowers, and is now being invaded by an alien empire called the Combined Army. Each player runs a small force of 8 to 15 very specialized models from one of the main factions, or from one of the dozens of "sectorial" sub-armies inside them.

Two ideas sit at the center of the game, and no other big skirmish game leans on them this hard.

**The order pool.** Every model on the table adds one Order to a shared pool at the start of your turn, and you spend those orders to activate models. You can pour every order into one specialist and run them across the board, spread them across the whole force, or anything in between. Lieutenants, hackers, and some specialists can make extra orders or take them away from the other player. Managing that pool is most of the game. You're always paying for the activation you want.

**ARO (Automatic Reaction Order).** When your model does something in an enemy's line of sight, that enemy gets to respond right then. Shoot back, dodge, hack, throw smoke, turn to face. Almost everything meaningful you do on your turn gets answered immediately, so moving across the table is a real decision, and the player whose turn it isn't is making choices on almost every order you spend. This is the thing that makes Infinity feel different the first time you play it.

Around those two sit the things you'd recognize from any modern miniatures game: cover, suppression fire, true line of sight through 3D terrain, camouflage and stealth markers, hacking, smoke, airborne deployment, doctors and engineers, and a deep pile of skills and equipment. Two lists at the same points can play completely differently.

The first edition came out in 2005. The current edition is **N5**, released at the end of 2024, which trimmed and rebalanced a lot of N4 but kept the order pool and ARO at the heart of it. A standard game is **300 points** on a heavily terrained 4 × 4 ft table, played over **three rounds**. The official recommendation is about 90 minutes, and real games (especially while you're learning) run longer.

## Why play it

Infinity is the skirmish game for people who want every decision to count. Lists are small enough that you know what every model on the table does, but the order pool means even a ten-model list gives you a turn full of choices. Which model burns the orders, which specialist you commit, when you spend the Lieutenant's order, when you throw a Coordinated Order at the line. There's no autopilot turn where you move everything six inches and pass.

The reactive turn is the part that hooks people. In most miniatures games the other player is mostly watching while you push your models around. In Infinity your opponent is right there with you on your turn, ARO after ARO, deciding whether to shoot, dodge, or wait for the next model coming around the corner. It's the most interactive miniatures game I've played, and probably the closest a tabletop wargame gets to a real-time strategy game.

The setting carries a lot of weight too. Hyper-corporations, religious states, a lost colony, a pan-Asian empire, anarchist motherships, a friendly superintelligent AI, and an alien invasion led by an Evolved Intelligence that absorbs whole species. The miniatures are some of the best in the industry, and the look is anime-influenced sci-fi (very Ghost in the Shell, Appleseed, and Masamune Shirow in general) without being on the nose about it.

The other thing people undersell: **the rules are free.** Every rule is on the official [Infinity Wiki](https://infinitythewiki.com/), and [Infinity Army](https://infinityuniverse.com/army/infinity), the official list builder, is free too. You can learn the game and build a list before you buy a single model.

## The factions

Each Infinity faction has its own setting identity, its own look, and a way of playing that carries across its sectorials. The playable factions in N5:

- **PanOceania.** The dominant superpower of the Human Sphere. High-tech, rich, a little religious, and usually the "heavy infantry with the best toys" faction. The TAGs (giant mechs) are iconic.
- **Yu Jing.** The pan-Asian StateEmpire, the other superpower. Mecha-samurai look, flexible to play, with strong heavy infantry and a good spread of specialists.
- **Ariadna.** Descendants of a lost colony ship that crashed on the planet Dawn. Lower-tech, mostly camo and infantry, with an in-universe excuse to put French legionnaires, Scottish highlanders, Cossacks, and US Rangers in one army. Cheap orders, lots of camo markers, and brutal up close.
- **Haqqislam.** A faction built around trade, biotech, and the best medicine in the Sphere. Versatile mid-tier infantry, excellent doctors, and the Hassassin assassins.
- **Nomads.** Three motherships full of dissidents, hackers, and exiles. The hacker faction. The look runs from grimy techwear to cybernetic body horror.
- **Combined Army.** The alien invasion, led by the Evolved Intelligence (EI) and made up of the species it has absorbed. Big alien monsters, possessed humans, and tools the human factions don't get.
- **ALEPH.** The Human Sphere's official superintelligence, fielded as physical avatars and Recreations of figures from Greek myth. Achilles, Ajax, and Hector all show up. Elite and expensive.
- **O-12.** The Sphere's international peacekeeping body, roughly the UN in space. A newer faction that plays like a high-tech intervention force.
- **JSA.** The Japanese Secessionist Army, which broke away from Yu Jing. Samurai, ninja, and yakuza.
- **Tohaa.** An old alien civilization whose warriors fight bonded to biotech symbionts, uneasy allies of humanity against the Combined Army. Still playable in N5, but the models are out of production.
- **Non-Aligned Armies.** Mercenaries, corporate security, and pirates who fight for whoever pays. Only sectorials here, like Druze Bayram Security, Ikari Company, and Dahshat Company.

Most of these have **sectorials**: smaller, focused armies (PanOceania's Military Orders, Yu Jing's Invincible Army, the Combined Army's Morat Aggression Force, and so on) that restrict what you can take in exchange for unique units, links, and a tighter identity. Most veteran players play sectorials.

The fastest way into N5:

1. **Pick a faction.** Browse the <a href="https://cbafflink.com/click/6a0b7b196bcc6347114d8cf3/250/subaccount/url=https%3A%2F%2Fstore.corvusbelli.com%2Fen%2Finfinity%2Fwargame%2Fstart-here" target="_blank" rel="sponsored nofollow noopener">Corvus Belli store</a> (our partner link, see above) by faction and find a look you like. You'll be painting these a lot, so the look matters as much as the rules.
2. **Get a starter box.** The cheapest way in is a two-player box. Corvus Belli has small **Infinity Essentials: Start Here** boxes for learning, and bigger **Battle Packs**, like Operation: Mazebreaker (ALEPH vs Nomads, 2026), with two forces in one box. If you already know your faction, each one has an **Action Pack** of about ten models.
3. **Read the rules for free.** Everything is on the [Infinity Wiki](https://infinitythewiki.com/). Start with orders, ARO, the face-to-face roll, and the basic skills. You don't need a hardback.
4. **Build a list on Infinity Army.** [Infinity Army](https://infinityuniverse.com/army/infinity) is the official list builder. It's free, runs in a browser, and stays current with N5.
5. **Play your first game small.** N5 has an official **150-point beginner** size on a 24 × 32 inch table, about 40 minutes. From there most people step up through 200 and 250 to the standard 300 once orders and ARO feel natural.

## How to get good

Get table time, and **play with the wiki open**. Infinity has one of the steeper learning curves of the big skirmish games, and a lot of that is just remembering what each model's skills do and how they interact. Looking things up mid-game is normal even for good players.

When you want to push past the basics:

- **Watch other people play.** [Robert Shepherd's YouTube channel](https://www.youtube.com/@R0bertShepherd) is where I'd start: battle reports, army reviews, and rules explainers. Watching an experienced player sequence their orders teaches more than rereading the rules.
- **Play one faction for a long time.** Most of the depth comes from knowing your sectorial cold: what every unit costs, what every skill does, what a good order spend looks like in the missions you play most. Hopping between factions slows that way down.
- **Learn the missions.** The competitive game is the ITS mission pack, which changes every season, and a lot of experienced players will tell you the missions decide more games than the lists do. Play the missions you'd see at a tournament.

Organized play runs on **ITS (the Infinity Tournament System)**, Corvus Belli's ranked global circuit. You can play at sanctioned events in person, or online on Tabletop Simulator with the community Infinity mod through the Infinity Global League. Even if you never plan to compete, a local ITS event is one of the fastest ways to find regular opponents.

## The Hobbinomicon take

Infinity is the game I keep coming back to when I want a skirmish where every order matters. I run Combined Army, the alien invasion faction, and it's one of the few full factions I'm holding onto through every purge. The sculpts are some of the most beautiful in the hobby, and the rules reward the kind of player who likes thinking three orders ahead.

The learning curve is real. For the first few games you spend more time looking up skills than playing. That's fine, that's normal, and the wiki is right there. Once orders and ARO click, no other skirmish game I've played has every single decision mattering like this.

If you bounced off Infinity before because the rules felt like too much, N5 is a good time to come back. It cleaned up a lot of the friction that built up through N3 and N4, and the starter boxes make getting in easier than it's ever been.
